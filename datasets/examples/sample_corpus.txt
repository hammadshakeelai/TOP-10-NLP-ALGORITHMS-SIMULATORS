Natural language processing turns messy human text into structured signals. Tokenization is often the first step because every downstream model depends on the boundaries and offsets it creates.

TF-IDF is a transparent vectorization method. It rewards terms that are common in one document and rare across the rest of the corpus, which makes it useful for ranking and retrieval demos.

Naive Bayes classifiers are simple probabilistic baselines. They combine class priors with word likelihoods and smoothing so learners can see how evidence accumulates.

Linear SVM classifiers work well with sparse text features. Their decision margins and feature coefficients make them useful for comparing positive and negative evidence.

RAKE extracts candidate phrases by splitting text on stopwords and punctuation. It scores words by frequency and degree, then sums word scores into ranked key phrases.

TextRank builds a graph from words or sentences. PageRank-style iteration assigns higher scores to nodes that are connected to other important nodes.

Word embeddings place terms in a vector space. Similar words tend to appear near each other, and analogy experiments reveal how vector arithmetic can encode relationships.

LSTM models process sequences one token at a time. Hidden state, cell state, and gates help demonstrate how recurrent networks carry information forward.

Transformer attention compares every token with every other token. Attention weights, masks, and positional encodings make the mechanism visible for short sequences.

Modern BERT, GPT, and T5 style models expose different transformer workflows. Masked prediction, generation, and text-to-text prompting can share a common simulator contract.
